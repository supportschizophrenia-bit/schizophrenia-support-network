Returning to Ourselves

Reflection from the eighth Schizophrenia Support Group Meeting

18th July 2026

We had our eighth support group meeting on last Saturday, and five of us came together. It was a thoughtful conversation that moved between pain, hope, nature, relationships, and the question of who we are beneath the identities we have carried for so long. We began by talking about our relationship with nature. One member shared that they had started going out again to photograph birds in the wetlands. They spoke about wanting to photograph flamingos, even though they are difficult to approach because of how easily they fly away. It was heartening to hear that they were leaving the house again, rediscovering an old passion, and slowly finding a sense of rhythm and structure in their days. Sometimes healing begins with something as simple as stepping outside and noticing the world again. As the conversation unfolded, we found ourselves reflecting on the ways we had been shaped by our families and by the expectations placed upon us since childhood. One person shared that they had spent much of their life trying to stay quiet, invisible, and responsible for everyone else's needs. Another spoke about always having to appear successful, beautiful, and strong, as though there was no room for struggle or vulnerability beneath the image others admired. I found myself recognising something similar in my own life—a part of me that learned to stay cheerful, make jokes, and protect itself from pain. Together, we wondered whether schizophrenia sometimes disrupts these identities that have held us for so long. While this can be frightening and deeply painful, it can also invite us to begin asking a different question: Who am I beneath all that I have been expected to be? A new member joined us for the first time and shared the immense loneliness of living with psychosis. They spoke about hearing voices telling them they should stop breathing or that they should not be alive. Medication had brought little relief, leaving them exhausted and searching for answers. As they described their relationship with their mother—the longing to be hugged, to be comforted, and to feel cared for—I could feel the depth of their loneliness. There are moments when another person's pain fills the room with such quietness that words seem almost too small. Sometimes the most meaningful thing we can offer is simply to remain present with one another. Later in the meeting, another member reflected on memories of sexual abuse and the pain of having those experiences denied. We also found ourselves exploring our relationship with validation—why we long for it, what it means when we do not receive it, and whether we can slowly begin to discover a sense of worth that grows from within as well as through our relationships with others. Towards the end of the meeting, one member shared that schizophrenia had changed them in unexpected ways. Having spent much of their childhood remaining quiet and accommodating, they now found themselves speaking more openly, setting boundaries, and expressing their own needs. They also shared a moving story about a friendship that had begun with a simple letter and grew into a relationship of mutual care and companionship. It was a reminder that even in lives marked by suffering, moments of deep human connection continue to find us. As the meeting came to a close, one person said they felt calmer and more at ease than when they had arrived. I left feeling a deep sense of belonging. We began by talking about birds, forests, and photographs, and somehow found ourselves speaking about identity, loneliness, trauma, friendship, and the slow journey of returning to ourselves. Perhaps healing is not about becoming someone new. Perhaps, over time, it is about gently finding our way back to the person we have always been beneath the layers of fear, expectation, and survival.

About these reflections

These reflections are written after each weekly Schizophrenia Support Network support group meeting. They are not transcripts, but thoughtful reflections on the themes that emerged during our conversations. Personal details have been removed or changed to protect confidentiality while preserving the humanity of what was shared.
