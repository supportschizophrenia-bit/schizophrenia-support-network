Weekly Schizophrenia Support Group

Every Saturday we gather online to create a space where people living with schizophrenia, psychosis, and related experiences can speak openly without judgement. Our conversations are grounded in respect, curiosity, and the belief that every person's experience deserves to be heard.

🌿 A Safe Space

Our meetings are confidential and non-judgemental. Participants are welcome to share whatever feels important—from voices, unusual beliefs, medication, relationships, trauma, loneliness, hope, or simply how their week has been.

These meetings are not lectures or therapy sessions. They are conversations between people who listen deeply, learn from one another, and understand that every experience deserves dignity and respect.

The group is facilitated by Pankaj Suneja

The Opposite of Shame Is Respect

This week we reflected on shame, dignity, belonging, and why respect itself can become a profound source of healing.

Read reflection →
27 June 2026

Five of us met last Saturday for our schizophrenia support group. Someone shared how difficult it can be to know whether a particular thought is simply part of being human or an early sign of psychosis returning. Another reflected on how psychosis had left them feeling disconnected from their body, and how healing has meant slowly becoming grounded again. Someone else spoke about spending much of life caring for others, only to realise they had lost touch with themselves in the process. We also reflected on trauma, the fear of breakdown, and what it means to preserve our humanity in a world that can often feel disconnected from kindness. By the end of the meeting, people shared that they felt at home. As I listened to everyone's stories, I found myself feeling a deep respect—for each person on the screen, in the room, and for myself. Out of that respect came a quiet sense of belonging. It felt precious, gently softening the shame that so often accompanies psychosis. Moments like these remind us that healing often begins when we no longer have to carry our stories alone.

20 June 2026

A few of us met yesterday for our schizophrenia support group on Zoom. We spoke about critical, threatening, and commanding voices, and explored how we might begin to build a different relationship with them. We reflected on how these voices can sometimes be connected to painful experiences from the past—times when similar messages were spoken by others and became deeply internalized. Rather than viewing these experiences only as symptoms, we tried to look beneath them and understand the human experiences, relationships, losses, and wounds that may lie underneath. We offered attention and significance to experiences that are often overlooked or dismissed, sometimes even within mental health services. One person shared how unlovable they feel in this world, and it touched my heart. Another shared how their voices threatened to kill them if they disclosed anything to a psychologist. Someone else spoke about experiences of abandonment in childhood and the attachment struggles that followed. Together, we explored the stigma that can accompany a diagnosis of schizophrenia and the additional burden that many people carry as a result. In one another's company, we experienced moments of connection, warmth, intimacy, quiet sadness, acceptance, and hope. We are grateful for the courage of everyone who participated. Thank you to everyone who joined yesterday's meeting and contributed to such a thoughtful discussion. 🙏 If you are living with schizophrenia and are looking for a space where your experiences can be shared, listened to, and explored with others, you are welcome to join us in future support group meetings

Community Values

Respect

Every person's experience deserves to be heard without judgement or ridicule.

Curiosity

We seek to understand before we attempt to explain, diagnose, or correct.

Confidentiality

Stories shared within the group remain within the group.

Hope

Recovery is not only about reducing symptoms but also about rebuilding meaning, connection, and dignity.
