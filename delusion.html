Phenomenology

Understanding Delusion: A Human Perspective

By Pankaj Suneja • Founder, Schizophrenia Support Network

Looking beyond symptoms and exploring delusions as meaningful responses to suffering, disruption, and the search for coherence.

What Happened to This Person's World?

When we encounter a person experiencing delusions, the first question is often: What symptom is this? We look for a diagnosis, categorize the belief, and assess whether it corresponds to reality.

While these questions have their place, another question is more important and humane: What happened to this person's world?

This question invites us to move beyond symptoms and enter the lived experience of the person. Rather than seeing a delusion merely as a false belief, we can begin to see it as a doorway into a person's inner life.

Delusion as Meaning-Making

Human beings are meaning-making creatures. We want to understand what is happening around us. A delusion can sometimes emerge as an attempt to explain experiences that otherwise feel incomprehensible.

The Emotional World Beneath Delusions

Beneath many delusions there may be emotional experiences that are difficult to tolerate directly. Terror, despair, loneliness, grief, shame, helplessness, rage, humiliation, abandonment, and profound vulnerability may all play a role.

Delusion as Communication

Conclusion

When we ask not only "What symptom is this?" but also "What happened to this person's world?", we begin to encounter the person behind the diagnosis.

Seen in this way, delusions are not merely objects to be eliminated. They are experiences to be understood. They invite us into the inner world of another human being and challenge us to respond not only with treatment, but also with compassion, curiosity, and respect.
