Understanding Schizophrenia

Exploring lived experience, phenomenology, and holistic perspectives

Understanding schizophrenia requires moving beyond medical labels to engage with the lived experience of those affected. Here, we gather resources from multiple perspectives to provide a fuller picture.

Resource Categories

📖 Lived Experiences & Phenomenology

First-person accounts and phenomenological explorations that center the subjective experience of schizophrenia.

Coming soon: Articles, personal narratives, and reflections from people with lived experience.

🧠 Symptomological Perspectives

Understanding hallucinations, delusions, thought disorders, negative symptoms, and cognitive changes—informed by phenomenology rather than pure medicalization.

Coming soon: Articles exploring what these experiences are like from the inside.

👥 Psychosocial Understanding

How social factors, relationships, trauma, life circumstances, and community impact schizophrenia and recovery.

Coming soon: Articles on family dynamics, workplace considerations, stigma, and social support.

💊 Physiological & Somatic Perspectives

Understanding the neurobiological, biochemical, and physical dimensions of schizophrenia—without reducing the person to pathology.

Coming soon: Articles on neurotransmitters, brain function, physical health, and medication considerations.

🔄 Recovery & Healing

Models of recovery, healing strategies, and pathways toward self-fulfillment and meaning.

Coming soon: Articles on recovery models, resilience, and moving toward purpose.

👨‍👩‍👧 Resources for Families

Support and education for family members and caregivers seeking to understand and support loved ones.

Coming soon: Articles for families, guides on communication, and caregiver self-care.

Our Approach to Resources

We focus on:

  • Phenomenology: What is it actually like to experience schizophrenia?
  • Holism: Understanding the whole person, not just symptoms
  • Dignity: Resources that respect the person, not just the diagnosis
  • Diversity: Multiple perspectives, experiences, and approaches to understanding
  • Accessibility: Clear, jargon-free language where possible
